Signs of a hidden leak

Most leaks don’t announce themselves.

If any of these sound familiar, it’s worth a conversation before anyone starts digging. A short call is usually enough to tell whether a leak investigation makes sense.

Unexpected water bills

Usage has climbed but nothing about how you use water has changed.

Meter moving with taps off

Everything is turned off, yet the meter keeps ticking over.

Damp ground or wet patches

Soft, green or soggy spots in the yard that never dry out.

A leak you can’t pinpoint

Damp walls, floors or concrete with no obvious source.

How we investigate

The right method depends on the pipe, the ground and the leak.

We don’t rely on a single tool. Each investigation uses a combination of methods chosen for your pipework and site conditions, explained in plain English as we go.

Method 01

Acoustic leak detection

Specialist listening equipment can help identify sounds produced by escaping water in pressurised pipework. It can be useful for underground and concealed leaks.

When is this used?
  • Pressurised water lines under lawns, paths or driveways
  • Concealed pipework inside walls or beneath floors
  • Often a first step to narrow down the search area

Site noise, depth, pipe materials and ground conditions can affect results.

Method 02

Nitrogen pressure testing

Nitrogen may be introduced as part of a controlled testing process to help assess pipework and assist with locating difficult leaks.

When is this used?
  • When conventional detection is inconclusive
  • To confirm whether a section of pipe is holding pressure
  • Alongside acoustic methods to make a quiet leak easier to hear

Suitability depends on the pipework and the ability to isolate sections safely.

Method 03

Tracer gas detection

A specialist tracer gas can be introduced into suitable pipework and detected with dedicated sensing equipment, helping to narrow down the location of certain concealed or underground leaks.

When is this used?
  • Small leaks that are too quiet to hear
  • Pipes under concrete, pavers or long runs of lawn
  • Where conditions allow the gas to reach the surface

Ground cover and soil type affect how the gas travels. We’ll explain what’s realistic for your site.

Method 04

Thermal imaging

A thermal camera detects surface temperature differences that may indicate moisture or a hidden plumbing issue, such as a hot water line leaking under a slab.

When is this used?
  • Warm spots in floors or damp patches on walls and ceilings
  • Hot water lines inside slabs or walls
  • To support findings from other methods

Thermal imaging supports an investigation but doesn’t on its own prove a leak or its exact location.

Method 05

Underground pipe & service locating

Electronic locating equipment can assist in tracing suitable underground services and identifying likely service routes, so repairs can be planned with less guesswork.

When is this used?
  • When nobody knows where the water service actually runs
  • Before a repair crew excavates or cuts concrete
  • Mapping routes on larger residential and commercial sites

Findings are indicative and should be verified as required before excavation or concrete cutting.

No single method suits every leak. We’ll tell you what we found, how confident we are, and what we recommend next.
