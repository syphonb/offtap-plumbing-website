How we investigate

Five methods. One clear answer.

Tap a card to see when each method is used. Most investigations combine two or more.

Acoustic detection

Listening for escaping water in pressurised pipes.

When is this used? ↻

When is this used?

  • Water lines under lawns, paths and driveways
  • Concealed pipes in walls and floors
  • Often the first step
Site noise, depth, pipe materials and ground conditions can affect results.

Nitrogen testing

Controlled pressure to assess pipework.

When is this used? ↻

When is this used?

  • When other methods are inconclusive
  • Checking whether a section holds pressure
  • Difficult or intermittent leaks
Depends on being able to isolate pipe sections safely.

Tracer gas

A safe gas rises through the ground at the leak.

When is this used? ↻

When is this used?

  • Small or quiet leaks
  • Pipes under concrete or pavers
  • Long runs of buried pipe
Ground cover and soil type affect how the gas travels.

Thermal imaging

Spotting temperature differences on surfaces.

When is this used? ↻

When is this used?

  • Warm spots in floors
  • Damp walls and ceilings
  • Hot water lines in slabs
Supports an investigation but doesn’t on its own prove a leak’s exact location.

Pipe locating

Tracing where underground services run.

When is this used? ↻

When is this used?

  • Unknown service routes
  • Before excavation or concrete cutting
  • Larger sites
Findings are indicative and should be verified before excavation.

No single method suits every leak. We’ll explain what we used, what we found and how confident we are.
