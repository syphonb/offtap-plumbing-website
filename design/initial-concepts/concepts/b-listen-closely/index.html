02 / How we investigate

Five ways to narrow it down.

Listening is one tool. Depending on the pipework and site, we combine methods to build a clearer picture before anything is dug up.

01

Acoustic detection

Listening for escaping water in pressurised lines.+
Best for
Pressurised water lines under lawns, paths, driveways or inside walls.
How
Specialist listening equipment picks up the sound of water escaping the pipe.
Limitations
Site noise, depth, pipe materials and ground conditions can affect results.
02

Nitrogen pressure testing

Controlled pressure to assess pipework.+
Best for
Difficult leaks, or when conventional detection is inconclusive.
How
Nitrogen is introduced in a controlled way to assess whether pipework holds pressure and to help locate the leak.
Limitations
Depends on being able to isolate sections of pipe safely.
03

Tracer gas detection

A safe gas that rises to where the leak is.+
Best for
Small or quiet leaks under concrete, pavers or long runs of lawn.
How
A specialist tracer gas is introduced into suitable pipework and detected at the surface with dedicated sensing equipment.
Limitations
Ground cover and soil type affect how the gas travels.
04

Thermal imaging

Seeing temperature differences on the surface.+
Best for
Warm spots in floors, damp walls and ceilings, hot water lines in slabs.
How
A thermal camera shows surface temperature differences that may indicate moisture or a hidden plumbing issue.
Limitations
Supports an investigation but doesn’t on its own prove a leak or its exact location.
05

Pipe & service locating

Tracing where the pipe actually runs.+
Best for
Unknown service routes, and planning before excavation or concrete cutting.
How
Electronic locating equipment helps trace suitable underground services and identify likely routes.
Limitations
Findings are indicative and should be verified before excavation.
