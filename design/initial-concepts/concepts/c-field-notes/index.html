STEP 01 · PIPE & SERVICE LOCATING

First, find where the pipe actually runs.

Electronic locating equipment can assist in tracing suitable underground services and identifying likely service routes.

When is this used?

When nobody is sure where the water service runs, and before any repair crew excavates or cuts concrete.

Findings are indicative and should be verified before excavation.

STEP 02 · ACOUSTIC DETECTION

Listen along the line.

Specialist listening equipment can help identify sounds produced by escaping water in pressurised pipework, useful for underground and concealed leaks.

When is this used?

Pressurised lines under lawns, paths, driveways or inside walls. Often the first step in narrowing down the search area.

Site noise, depth, pipe materials and ground conditions can affect results.

STEP 03 · NITROGEN PRESSURE TESTING

Confirm what’s holding pressure.

Nitrogen may be introduced as part of a controlled testing process to help assess pipework and assist with locating difficult leaks.

When is this used?

As an additional method when conventional detection is inconclusive, or to confirm which section of pipe is losing pressure.

STEP 04 · TRACER GAS & THERMAL IMAGING

Narrow it down further.

A specialist tracer gas can be detected at the surface where conditions allow. A thermal camera shows temperature differences that may indicate moisture or a hidden hot water leak.

When is this used?

Quiet leaks, pipes under concrete or pavers, and warm or damp spots inside the home.

Thermal imaging supports an investigation but does not on its own prove a leak’s exact location.

STEP 05 · FINDINGS

Mark it, explain it, plan the repair.

We explain what we found and, where possible, mark the suspected location or provide information to help the repair. That means less guesswork and less unnecessary digging.
